## Overview

Investigation of 3D-printed lattice structures as novel wick geometries for heat pipe applications. Traditional sintered powder or mesh wicks impose manufacturing constraints on heat pipe geometry — particularly for flexible configurations needed in spacecraft. This thesis explored whether additive manufacturing could produce lattice-structured wicks with controllable capillary and permeability characteristics suited for spacecraft thermal systems, in continuation of the ISRO flexible heat pipe design work.

## Problem Statement

Conventional heat pipe wicks (sintered powder, mesh, axial grooves) are difficult to manufacture in flexible or complex geometries. Lattice structures fabricated via additive manufacturing offer:
- Tunable porosity and pore geometry through design
- Consistent, reproducible capillary characteristics
- Compatibility with flexible heat pipe configurations

## Methodology

**Lattice design:** Multiple lattice topologies evaluated — BCC (Body-Centred Cubic), FCC (Face-Centred Cubic), and octet-truss — parametrically modeled in SolidWorks. Strut diameter and unit cell size varied to control porosity and effective pore radius.

**Fabrication:** FDM and SLA additive manufacturing used to produce wick samples. Print parameters optimized for dimensional accuracy of fine lattice features.

**Flexible adiabatic section:** Steel-braided flexible adiabatic section designed and fabricated to connect evaporator and condenser sections while allowing bending — addressing the key geometric constraint in spacecraft applications.

**Experimental characterization:**
- Capillary rise testing to measure effective pore radius and wicking performance
- Thermal performance evaluation across lattice topologies
- Comparison against conventional wick structures

## Key Contributions

- First characterization of FDM/SLA-printed lattice wicks for heat pipe applications at NIT Trichy
- Design and fabrication of a functioning flexible heat pipe prototype with lattice wick and braided adiabatic section
- Parametric data relating lattice geometry to capillary and thermal performance
