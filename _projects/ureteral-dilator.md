## Overview

Reverse-engineering of a patent-expired ureteral dilator system for mass manufacturing, commissioned by a medical device manufacturing client seeking to eliminate supply-chain dependencies on foreign OEM parts. The scope covered the full component set of the dilator system — 10+ individual parts — from reference measurement through production-ready design and prototype validation.

**Outcome:** 10+ functional designs accurate to ±25 microns, with full GD&T production drawings and SLA-printed prototypes in FDA-approved polymers delivered for client approval.

## Design Process

**Requirements capture:** Shortlisted critical design requirements and technical specifications from client brief and clinical context. Identified non-negotiable features (lumen geometry, deployment mechanics, material biocompatibility) versus parameters open to redesign.

**Patent study:** Reviewed expired patents to understand original design intent and identify features that must be preserved for functional equivalence versus those that could be modified or optimised for manufacturability.

**Reverse engineering:** Measured reference devices using digital calipers, CMM where available, and optical comparison. Reconstructed 3D geometry in Fusion 360 to ±25 micron accuracy across all critical dimensions.

**Design modifications:** Incorporated client's manufacturing preferences and supply-chain constraints into the redesigned components — adjusting draft angles, wall thicknesses, and feature geometries for compatibility with their production processes.

**Prototyping:** All components designed and prototyped in SLA using FDA-approved biocompatible polymers (Class I/II). Prototypes used for functional validation and client approval of form, fit, and function before committing to production tooling.

**Deliverables:** Full Fusion 360 assembly and part files, GD&T production drawings for each component, and a physical prototype set.

## Tools

Fusion 360 · SLA 3D Printing · GD&T · Digital measurement / CMM
